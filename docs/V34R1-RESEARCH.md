# V34-R1 improvement research: standard-codec task-aware preprocessing

Date: 2026-10-15. Read-only review of the source checkout; no training, dataset download, repository edit, or publication was performed for this note. The proposals below are hypotheses to measure, not results or promises of a BD-rate target.

## What the current evidence establishes

The current pipeline is sender-side region filtering followed by real x264/x265 encoding, independent standard decoding, and frozen task models. It does not train the codec or RGB preprocessor end to end. The controller learns measured action/rate/risk tables. The existing 13 actions only mix two Gaussian frequency bands with six region coefficients; the region maps have object/history, motion/context, and background channels. See [PIPELINE](../docs/PIPELINE.md), [actions](../adaptive_vcm/v34/protocol.py), [filter](../adaptive_vcm/v34/preprocessing.py), and [candidate measurement](../adaptive_vcm/v34/runner.py).

The published primary AR estimates are approximately −14.46% H.264 and −11.41% H.265, with confidence intervals extending well above −15%. OD is substantially weaker under shape-preserving interpolation: the full five-QP primary forced arm reports −2.54% / −2.64% PCHIP BD-rate, despite H.264 cubic reporting −22.15%. Its four-QP readout is −9.99% / −3.63%. These are repository measurements, not literature results. Dropping QP 50 after examining TEST cannot constitute a new prospective primary protocol. No published OT result directory is present in the reviewed `results/` tree; source-track consistency on K400 is not GT tracking accuracy. See [README](../README.md), [OD report](../results/v34_od_coco/REPORT.md), and [evaluation scope](../adaptive_vcm/v34/evaluation.py).

The code's existing success criterion is obsolete for this request: [curve_summary](../adaptive_vcm/metrics.py) checks point estimates below −10%; [holdout.merge](../adaptive_vcm/v34/holdout.py) additionally requires the primary CI upper endpoint below **zero**. Neither proves strictly below −15%.

## Primary evidence and its limits

* Lu et al., *Preprocessing Enhanced Image Compression for Machine Vision*, arXiv 2022 / TCSVT 2024, place a QP-adaptive neural preprocessor before a conventional codec and preserve a fixed downstream model. Their study supports richer preprocessing and QP conditioning. Its preprint evaluates BPG/JPEG, image detection/classification, and explicitly limits transfer claims; it does not establish this repository's H.264/H.265 AR/OT result. Their neural training recipe uses a proxy codec and substantial training, whereas V34 uses cached real measurements. [Author paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2206.05650), [publisher record](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/10632166/).
* Różek et al., *Video coding for machines using region-of-interest-based retargeting*, 2025, describe protected RoIs, low-pass background simplification, and optional graying, then a separate geometry-changing retargeting stage. Their VVC/VCM-RS study motivates stronger simplification; its gains cannot be assigned to a geometry-preserving x264/x265 filter. [Primary article, §§4–6](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s13640-025-00682-3). The authors' MPEG contribution includes implementation material. [Primary contribution m62235](https://www.multimedia.edu.pl/publications/m62235-VCM-CE16---RoI-based-simplification-and-retargeting-for-Video--Coding-for-Machines.pdf).
* Windsheimer, Brand and Kaup, *On Annotation-free Optimization of Video Coding for Machines*, compare task predictions on compressed images with predictions on their clean sources as pseudo ground truth. Their learned-image-codec segmentation experiment motivates source-only task loss and separates compression damage from source-model errors. It does not make teacher consistency a guarantee of GT task accuracy. [Author paper, §3](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.07938v1).
* Ge et al., *Task-Aware Encoder Control for Deep Video Compression*, CVPR 2024, control frozen learned-video-codec components using task loss, mode choice, and GoP choice. Their comparison of hand-crafted, exhaustive, and learned selection motivates measuring search-space and selector gaps separately. Their latent-mode tools are not a drop-in x264/x265 preprocessor. [Author paper, §§3.2–3.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.04848v1).

## Five concrete improvements worth testing

### 1. Broaden simplification while preserving geometry

**Code bottleneck:** [filter_clip](../adaptive_vcm/v34/preprocessing.py) is limited to σ=0.8/1.6 frequency edits with lower coefficient −0.75. It cannot fully suppress high frequency or simplify larger-scale background structure. Increasing controller epochs cannot overcome an inadequate action table.

**Proposed experiment:** append a small, versioned action family with stronger multiscale background low-pass filtering, independently bounded context strength, and protected object cores. Include a few conservative low-pass-only actions and stronger background candidates; preserve identity exactly and retain output dimensions and integer geometry. Consider chroma attenuation or mean-color background as separately named, higher-risk candidates, never assume them harmless to AR context. This is an engineering extrapolation from Lu's expressive preprocessing and Różek's simplification, not a reproduction of either method. Freeze the family and parameters on TRAIN/DEV before TEST.

**Ablation:** original 13 versus expanded actions under the same source guard, first using exhaustive TRAIN/DEV measurements. Record the extra minimum feasible rate and GT-task effects; distinguish an action-space ceiling from learned-selection error. Only later compare shortlists at equal trial budgets. Adding retargeting would require a different packet/geometry contract and paid metadata and should be a separate study.

### 2. Make OD source-only loss real, and align cache targets with deployment

**Correctness prerequisite:** [Heads.costs](../adaptive_vcm/v34/tasks.py) respects `source_only` for AR and OT, but line 102 enters the OD GT-loss branch whenever `r.task == 'od'`. A source-only OD guard would therefore access `r.targets` even though [evaluation](../adaptive_vcm/v34/evaluation.py) deliberately creates a source record without GT. Existing base OD settings do not enable that guard, so this is a dormant defect, not evidence that published OD results used TEST GT.

**Proposed experiment:** route `source_only=True` through source detections or source pseudo-target task loss for OD; keep GT targets in training/scoring records only. Cache both source-only damage and GT damage as separate fields with provenance, then train the deployed shortlist primarily on the objective it will encounter. Keep GT damage as supervised evidence and an ablation. For a detection-matching guard, penalize unmatched candidate detections, localization changes, and score changes; the current [detection_distance](../adaptive_vcm/v34/tasks.py) largely ignores extra candidate boxes when source references exist. Windsheimer et al. motivate the pseudo-target objective; the specific loss and thresholds remain to be validated.

**Ablation:** GT-trained ranker versus source-trained ranker with the identical decoded source guard, then box-only versus pseudo-target task loss. Report source-risk/GT-error correlation and failures on clean-source mistakes, not only averaged source loss.

### 3. Protect weak tracked objects and validate temporal boundaries

**Code bottlenecks:** [region_maps](../adaptive_vcm/v34/preprocessing.py) discards scores below .25, while the [ByteTrack adapter](../adaptive_vcm/v34/tracking.py) consumes person detections above .1. Thus valid low-score tracker observations can lie in heavily filtered background. History is also rebuilt for each `region_maps` call. [Heads.costs](../adaptive_vcm/v34/tasks.py) runs a fresh tracker for each candidate window, while receiver evaluation maintains trackers over windows.

ByteTrack's central mechanism uses low-score detections associated with existing tracklets to recover occluded objects, supporting protection of associated weak observations rather than every weak box indiscriminately. [Author paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.06864), [official implementation](https://github.com/FoundationVision/ByteTrack).

**Proposed experiment:** add confidence-aware, causal support for low-score observations associated with source tracklets; carry support history across adjacent windows with explicit scene/sequence resets. For the guard, evaluate each candidate from an identical cloned past tracker state and update the committed state once after selection. Check receiver boundary ID switches separately. These changes are hypotheses based on the adapter mismatch, not guaranteed gains from the ByteTrack paper.

**Ablation:** .25 box masks versus tracked low-confidence support; reset versus persistent guard state; report crowded/occlusion subsets and boundary switches. Do not introduce future-frame unions into a pipeline described as causal.

### 4. Learn feasible candidate ranking, not only one hard oracle label

**Code bottlenecks:** [targets](../adaptive_vcm/v34/learning.py) collapses each measured table to one action label using rate plus .25 times raw mean task damage. Risk tasks have different units. [choose_packet](../adaptive_vcm/v34/evaluation.py) tries only three nonidentity actions, ranked with another raw mean-risk score. Once a good candidate is excluded, the real source guard cannot recover it.

**Proposed experiment:** retain the measured feasible set and per-action regret, weight each source equally across its QP/codec rows, and train a soft or pairwise ranking target over feasible candidates. Use task-tolerance-normalized risk in selection diagnostics and retain measured rate as the final objective. Build a diverse shortlist containing the best rate prediction and distinct strength/family candidates, plus identity. Ge et al.'s exhaustive-versus-learned selection experiment motivates this decomposition; it does not prove a particular ranking loss superior here.

**Ablation:** hard CE versus feasible-set ranking; shortlist sizes 1/3/6/all; pure-rate versus risk-normalized versus diverse ranking. Report feasible-best recall@K, selected-rate regret, safety failures, identity fallback, actual encoder trials, and wall time. An exhaustive source-only selector can be a deployed upper-compute arm; a GT oracle is a diagnostic TRAIN/DEV bound only.

### 5. Calibrate the deployed policy at the independent-source level

**Code bottleneck:** [calibrate](../adaptive_vcm/v34/learning.py) takes per-action empirical residual quantiles, treating correlated codec/QP rows as calibration observations. [calibrate_domains](../adaptive_vcm/v34/learning.py) discloses correlation but does not remove it. In source-guard mode the calibrated action is only logged; it is not a veto. Changing its coverage alone does not constrain the selected arm.

Learn then Test formulates risk-control calibration as multiple hypothesis testing for a frozen family, allowing simultaneous risks under its stated sampling assumptions. It provides a useful framework for certifying a complete frozen selection policy rather than assuming marginal action residuals certify adaptive selection. [Author paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.01052), [official implementation](https://github.com/aangelopoulos/ltt).

**Proposed experiment:** tune thresholds/ranking on DEV, freeze the full policy, and evaluate a predeclared small policy family on independent CAL sources. Aggregate correlated conditions per source or sequence according to the declared risk, with multiplicity control if claiming statistical risk certification. Increase CAL substantially beyond 16 AR/OD sources and avoid sequence overlap for OT. Report empirical coverage and subgroup failures even when formal assumptions are not supported. Do not claim distribution-free GT guarantees from unbounded CE losses, correlated windows, source-only scores, or domain shift.

## Block-level validation before a long run

| Block | Necessary check and saved evidence |
|---|---|
| Source/split | Dataset membership, pixel/ID hashes, source-sequence grouping, true cadence, geometry and labels; no padded or reused TEST replacement; distinct TRAIN/CAL/DEV/TEST manifests. |
| Region/support | Shape, finite nonnegative maps summing to one; clipped boxes; weak tracked object coverage; causal history and scene-cut/sequence resets; window-boundary trace. |
| Filtering | Exact identity; finite uint8 RGB; unchanged shape/coordinates; bounded parameters; saved before/after residuals and object/context/background edit magnitudes; no filter-family reuse under an old checkpoint schema. |
| Codec | Real x264/x265 with frozen versions/options; same anchor/adaptive QP, preset, pixel format, GOP and decoded shape; bitstream and decoded hashes; all paid header/geometry metadata included; independent decoder roundtrip. |
| Tasks/guard | Frozen weights/transforms; deterministic proposal sampling; source-only OD succeeds with GT fields absent; GT permutation/removal does not change chosen packets; identical tracker past state for every trial; all active tasks checked. |
| Controller/cache | Cache keyed by source/action/filter/teacher/codec versions; measured rate/damage schema and masks; no TEST rows in optimization/calibration; ranking recall and residuals by task/codec/QP; parameter changes only during TRAIN. |
| Selection | Identity always feasible; finite validated limits/budget; minimum actual bytes among feasible tried candidates; exact fallback reasons; candidate work and state-commit accounting; shared selected packet for task scoring. |
| Evaluation | Complete paired grid, predictions/packets fixed before GT scoring; official metrics, common overlap, monotonicity/reliability checks, paired uncertainty, immutable protocol and output manifest. |

These checks should include focused synthetic contract tests, real-codec integration, and a small predeclared TRAIN/DEV smoke run. Passing code tests does not establish task accuracy or BD-rate.

## Requirements for an honest strictly-below-−15% result

1. **Freeze the claim.** Treat AR×H.264, AR×H.265, OD×H.264, OD×H.265, OT×H.264 and OT×H.265 as six endpoints if both codecs must pass for every task. Predeclare one model/seed or ensemble, AR top-1, OD COCO AP@[.50:.95], OT HOTA, dataset membership, rate accounting, QP grid, interpolation, overlap rule and all exclusions. Average gains across tasks/codecs do not satisfy an each-endpoint threshold.
2. **Use official task scores.** Evaluate OD with official COCO evaluation and OT against annotated IDs with pinned TrackEval settings and ignore-region treatment. TrackEval is the authors' HOTA reference and also provides identity/CLEAR metrics. Save HOTA/DetA/AssA, IDF1 and MOTA, along with raw tracking files. The repository's [tracking_cost](../adaptive_vcm/v34/tracking.py) explicitly is a local surrogate. [Official TrackEval](https://github.com/JonathonLuiten/TrackEval).
3. **Make BD valid before making it favorable.** Predeclare a shape-preserving primary interpolator (PCHIP is defensible), with cubic sensitivity for continuity with historical results; preserve all raw points. Require positive rates, enough distinct task-quality points, a nontrivial common quality interval, no extrapolation, and monotone/reliable curves or a predeclared constrained fitting protocol. Do not sort nonmonotone qualities into an apparent valid RD relation or choose cubic/PCHIP/QP subsets after seeing which passes. BD literature documents overlap, saturation and interpolation sensitivity and supports examining relative curve differences and additional supporting points when needed. [Herglotz et al., §§III,V](https://arxiv.org/html/2304.12852v2), [authors' implementation](https://github.com/FAU-LMS/bjontegaard).
4. **Distinguish threshold attainment from threshold confidence.** A strict point pass is `bd_rate_pct < -15.0`, not `<= -15`, rounding to −15.00, or a CI below zero. A statistical claim that each endpoint saves at least 15% requires each one-sided simultaneous upper confidence bound strictly below −15.0. For six fixed endpoints, a conservative choice is one-sided Bonferroni level .05/6; two-sided intervals can use tail .05/(2×6). State which claim is being made and control extra primary model/seed choices if any.
5. **Bootstrap independent units with the full metric recomputed.** AR: paired videos, preserving all conditions/arms. OD: paired image resampling, rebuild/renumber repeated image IDs and recompute dataset-level COCO AP each draw; AP is not a mean of per-image APs. OT: independent sequences, recomputing aggregate TrackEval sufficient statistics/metrics; windows from one sequence are not independent sequences. Refit BD within each draw. Save draw seed/count, finite fraction and invalid reasons; predeclare a high validity fraction and fail closed when it is not met. The exact CI construction is an engineering/statistical design recommendation, not a ready-made guarantee from the codec papers.
6. **Keep independent evidence independent.** The old 1,024-video holdout and 64-image COCO TEST have already been inspected. Repeated optimization against them is development. They can be regression sets, but a confirmatory v34r1 claim needs a new frozen holdout or clearly disclosed reused-test status. Show same-QP quality/rate, per-source regressions, latency and uncertainty, including failures, alongside BD.

## Data and compute constraints

* The reviewed repository ships cached results and controller weights, not source video datasets or pretrained task weights. Availability of K400/COCO/MOT data on the current host was not independently audited in this research task. [Reproduction instructions](../docs/REPRODUCE.md) require external K400 frames, COCO annotations, MOT frames/GT, FFmpeg codecs, torchvision task weights and TrackEval for a new run.
* The existing K400 experiment uses provider-predecoded JPEGs with unverified source cadence, not verified original full-video evaluation. The base AR input is 128 pixels, OD 320 pixels, OT 960 pixels in the R1 recipe. This scope must accompany any claims.
* The current OT split gives one DEV and one TEST sequence and splits MOT20-02 windows between TRAIN and CAL. This is useful for debugging but insufficient for credible generalization uncertainty across independent scenes. Adding many adjacent windows does not solve that problem. MOT20's official archive lists only four training sequences; planning a larger disjoint-sequence study may require another annotated dataset or a clearly limited claim. Its benchmark submission/evaluation service is currently closed, although archived data/results remain available. [Official MOT20 archive](https://motchallenge.net/data/MOT20/).
* Real candidate measurement scales linearly with sources × codecs × QPs × actions, plus frozen task analysis. With the current AR+OT recipe, 64 AR TRAIN/CAL sources plus 16 MOT windows, 2 codecs, 5 QPs and 13 actions imply 10,400 offline candidate roundtrips before evaluation. This count follows the local recipe; it is not a measured runtime. Enlarging the action family or shortlist has a direct compute cost. Benchmark all candidates honestly rather than inferring speed from 8,661 controller parameters.
* The existing independent AR run reports roughly 4.5–5.2 hours per 256-video shard on its historical environment. A new richer search, OD bootstrap, and higher-resolution OT study can take much longer. Lu et al.'s full neural preprocessor training is also a different and materially larger compute program; it should not be represented as a quick controller patch.

Recommended order: fix source-only OD and temporal/state contracts; measure expanded action feasibility on TRAIN/DEV; measure shortlist recall/compute; train the selected small controller change; freeze calibration and protocol; only then run all six fresh endpoint evaluations. Publish code and measured limitations if any target fails. Do not manufacture a success claim or reinterpret source consistency as GT evidence.
